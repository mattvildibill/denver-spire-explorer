Controls

Drag to look around. Use WASD or the arrow keys to move; on a phone, use the left joystick. Fly adds Q / E to lower or rise, plus scrolling to move forward. Hold Shift to move faster.

Choose a landmark to jump there. Walk keeps you at street level with building collision checks. R returns to Spire; Esc stops a tour or restores the panels.

Open 2D overview

Graphics

Phones and tablets start in Light mode. Changing quality reloads the explorer. Light keeps all landmarks and mapped buildings with simpler decorative detail; High adds photo materials and district reflections.

How I built it

I built this explorer from Denver’s open building records and aerial imagery, then added walking, landmark tours and an offline edition. Source geometry stays separate from inferred architectural detail. The lighter rendering profile reduces memory use while keeping every mapped building and destination.

Model details & limitations

What is accurate?

The model contains 530 building groups and 1,916 roof sections across about 2.6 km². City roof outlines and height records preserve real scale, placement, and true north. Spire has the most architectural detail, including its podium, stepped crown, floor slabs and approximate balcony modules.

Materials & light

Photographed concrete, asphalt and brick; afternoon environment lighting; baked geometric ambient shading; and reflections of the modeled district. Spire mullions, window surrounds and curbs have physical depth. Close-range architectural modules are inferred, not surveyed.

What is approximate?

This is an explorable GIS reconstruction, not a perfect photogrammetric replica. Facades, balconies and some trees and lamps are procedural or inferred. Ground is level. Detailed sculptures, interiors, current storefronts, street furniture and the rebuilt 16th Street Mall are not fully reconstructed. Aerial imagery is from September 2023 and contains shadows and building lean; it is not a current survey.

Real Street View opens Google's own photography at the current camera location in a separate tab or app. It requires no API key from you. Google may show its own consent screen; coverage and date vary. No Google, Instagram or commercial map imagery is copied into this model.

    About this project & its sources

    I built this small, explorable model of downtown Denver from open city data. The 3D edition adds street-level walking, a six-stop tour and a self-contained offline download.

    City roof mapping principally dates to 2022. The full model uses September 2023 aerial imagery. Facades and some architectural details are inferred; ground is level. This is an educational reconstruction, not a current survey or a route-planning tool.
